# Labelling the Blind Pools

Every test in [objects-plan.md](objects-plan.md) is scored by labelling a shuffled pool of entries from numbered contact sheets, before any key or catalogue is opened. This is the rubric for those labels from the eighth test on. Earlier tests used the same three verdicts without anything written down, so a second labeller can judge with this page alone.

Each entry gets one verdict, with a detail after a dash where one fits: I (worth a look), O (ordinary) or A (artefact). If an entry fits more than one, the first that applies in the order below wins.

## The Two Kinds of Entry

A main entry is one colour stamp of a whole system, with F444W as red, F277W as green and F150W as blue.

A compact entry is a four-panel tile 2.4" across, with the candidate at the centre of each panel. Top left is F277W, top right F444W, bottom left the same three-colour view, bottom right F115W, and each panel is stretched to its own centre.

## Artefacts

An entry is an artefact when what sits at the centre isn't a source on the sky, or is a star.

- A-stripe: a straight band of striping, or a satellite trail, crosses the centre or makes up the candidate. A real source with a stripe across it counts here only if the stripe passes through its centre.
- A-spike: a diffraction spike or halo of a bright star.
- A-piece: a clump of a larger galaxy, or light at the edge of a bright galaxy's halo, rather than a source of its own.
- A-pixel: a dot one pixel sharp in one or two bands, a hot pixel or cosmic ray.
- A-move: two or three like dots in a short line, each in different bands or different colours, from something moving between exposures.
- A-edge: the edge of the image, a gap or a ghost.
- A-noise: nothing at the centre stands out from the noise around it in the bands where it should show.
- A-star: a point source bright in F115W and F150W and fading to F444W, blue or white in the colour view. A point brown dwarf, faint in F150W and bright in F444W, is not a star for this.

## Worth a Look

An entry is worth a look when an astronomer going through a survey would stop and want to know more.

- A main stamp is worth a look for tidal tails, bridges or shapes disturbed by an interaction, a ring, an arc or anything lensed, a one-sided tail (a tadpole), a point nucleus much brighter than its host, or colours that change sharply across the object, such as clumps of very different colour.
- In a main stamp, colour alone isn't enough. A red spiral, a red edge-on disc or a red elliptical with nothing else odd about it is ordinary, as dusty or distant galaxies are common at these depths.
- A compact tile is worth a look for a point or near point that is red (much brighter in F444W than F150W), green (bright in F277W alone, a strong line), or seen in the long-wave bands with nothing in F115W (a dropout), and any brown dwarf.

## Ordinary

Everything else is ordinary, such as a normal galaxy of any shape, a close pair without signs of interacting, a small source with ordinary colours (white or yellow in the colour view), or a faint point whose colour can't be told.
